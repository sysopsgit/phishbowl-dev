"CEO Wire Transfer" BEC Attack

High Severity
From: John Smith <john.smith@company-mall.com> SUSPICIOUS
Subject: Urgent wire transfer needed
To: you@yourcompany.com

Hey,

I'm in a board meeting and can't take calls right now. Need you to handle something important ASAP.

We need to send a wire transfer of $24,500 to a vendor who's been waiting on payment. I'll send the bank details once you confirm you can process this today.

Text me on +1 (555) 123-4567 — it's my personal cell for today since I'm out of office.

Thanks,
John Smith
CEO, YourCompany Inc.

Red Flags to Look For

!

Impersonation of Executive (BEC)

Business Email Compromise. The attacker pretends to be the CEO/CFO. Note the fake domain "company-mall.com" — attackers register lookalike domains.

!

Urgent Financial Request

"Can't take calls" + "wire money now" is a textbook red flag. Always verify wire transfer requests through a second channel (a phone call, a known number).

!

Requests Communication Outside Official Channels

Asking you to text a personal cell number is a way to bypass company security policies and monitoring. Always use official communication channels.

!

Avoids Standard Process

"Send bank details later" — real financial processes follow strict approval workflows. No legitimate executive asks to bypass procurement/payment procedures.

!

Lookalike Domain

"company-mall.com" mimics a real company domain. Always check the exact spelling of the sender's domain before acting on sensitive requests.
